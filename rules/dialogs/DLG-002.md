## Rule

Never open a dialog on top of another dialog. Show any further step, including a question about discarding typed input, by replacing the open dialog's content, and a failed main action as a notice inside it, or close the dialog and move the work to a page. Only a full-screen dialog may have a short confirmation or alert on top.

## Why

A dialog already takes the person away from the page and hides part of it. A second dialog on top hides even more, and the person has to keep two unfinished things in mind at once. They also have to close the layers in the right order to get back. On the web, each layer holds focus, so two layers mean two places where focus can get lost when one closes. One dialog at a time keeps the path short: in, do the task, out. No WCAG criterion forbids a second dialog. [WCAG 2.2, 2.4.3 Focus Order](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#focus-order), [2.1.2 No Keyboard Trap](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#no-keyboard-trap) and [4.1.2 Name, Role, Value](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#name-role-value) are all easier to meet with one layer than with two. This rule is our design choice.

## Do

- A dialog *Edit profile* has a button *Choose department* that needs a long list. The list replaces the dialog's content, with a *Back* button that brings back the profile fields as the person left them.
- A flow to add a new user has three steps. Each step replaces the last inside the same dialog, and the dialog shows "Step 2 of 3".
- The person has typed in a dialog *New note* and presses *Cancel*. The dialog asks first, as the guideline FRM-004b describes: its content is replaced by the question "Discard this note?" with *Cancel* and *Discard note*. *Discard note* names the action, as DLG-003 says, and uses the danger style, as DLG-005 says. Focus starts on *Cancel*, as DLG-004 says. *Cancel* brings back the note as the person left it and puts focus on the *Cancel* button they pressed.
- *Save* in a dialog *Edit customer* fails because a colleague changed the same customer a minute ago. Add a notice inside the dialog, above the buttons, such as "Someone else changed this customer while you were typing. Copy what you want to keep, then reload." Keep everything the person typed.
- A form that holds a lot of typed input lives on a page, or in a full-screen dialog on a small screen.
- On a phone, the person fills in a damage report for their car in a full-screen dialog *Damage report*, then closes it. One short confirmation opens over it: "Discard the damage report?" with *Cancel* and *Discard report*. *Cancel* closes the confirmation and puts focus back on the control in the full-screen dialog that opened it.
- While that confirmation is open, the dialog beneath cannot be reached with the keyboard, the mouse or a screen reader. Only the top layer is marked as a modal dialog, and it has its own role and name. Escape does what *Cancel* does, so it closes only the confirmation.
- The main action succeeds. Close the dialog and show the result as a message on the page, such as "Profile saved", not as another dialog.

## Don't

- Open *Choose department* as a second dialog on top of *Edit profile*.
- Open each step of a flow as a new dialog over the one before.
- Open "Discard this note?" as a second dialog on top of an ordinary dialog. Replace the dialog's content instead.
- Show "Someone else changed this customer" in an alert over an ordinary dialog.
- Show a field's error, such as "Enter a valid phone number", in a dialog over a form dialog. It belongs at the field, as FRM-002a says.
- Let a confirmation over a full-screen dialog offer a new task, such as *Save as template …* that opens a further dialog.
- Show two confirmations over a full-screen dialog at the same time.

## Exceptions

- **One short confirmation over a full-screen dialog.** A full-screen dialog covers the whole screen and is meant for small screens. One short confirmation or alert about it may open on top, but only when closing it would throw away what the person entered, or when its main action failed for a reason not tied to a field, such as a clash with someone else's change. It holds no task of its own, opens nothing further, and returns focus to the full-screen dialog when it closes. Never show two at once. Field errors are never covered by this exception. What a discard confirmation says, where its focus starts and how it looks are DLG-003, DLG-004 and DLG-005.
- **Menus, pickers and popovers.** A dropdown menu, a date picker, a tooltip or a popover that opens from a control inside a dialog is not a dialog, and this rule does not cover it.
- **Non-modal panels.** A side panel that leaves the page usable is not a modal dialog and is outside this rule.
- **What Escape does.** Escape does what Cancel does, as DLG-001 says. In a discard question that replaced the dialog's content, it brings back the content. In a confirmation over a full-screen dialog, it closes only the confirmation.
