## Rule

While a modal dialog is open, Tab and Shift+Tab stay within it, and nothing behind it can be used until it closes.

## Why

A modal dialog says: answer this first. A sighted mouse user gets that message from the dimmed page. A keyboard user gets it only if Tab stays in the dialog. If Tab walks on into the page behind, focus disappears under the dialog or its backdrop, and the person may change something they cannot see while the question is still waiting. No WCAG criterion requires a modal dialog to hold focus, so the judges decide this rule. The law permits it and is easier to meet with it. Holding focus is not a trap under [WCAG 2.2, 2.1.2 No Keyboard Trap](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#no-keyboard-trap) as long as the dialog can be closed from the keyboard, which DLG-001 and KBD-001 see to. Focus that never goes behind the dialog cannot be hidden by it, which helps meet [2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum)](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#focus-not-obscured-minimum), and a focus order that stops at the dialog's edge supports [2.4.3 Focus Order](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#focus-order). What a dialog tells assistive technology about itself should be true, as [4.1.2 Name, Role, Value](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#name-role-value) asks of every state, and a dialog announced as modal is only truthful when the page behind is out of use, so the rule supports that criterion too. How focus gets in and where it goes afterwards is DLG-006.

## Do

- In a meal planner, the dialog *Plan a meal* has a close icon, *Day*, *Recipe*, *Servings*, *Cancel* and *Add to plan*. Tab on *Add to plan* goes to the close icon, the first control in the dialog. Shift+Tab on the close icon goes to *Add to plan*.
- Behind the dialog, the week's grid, the shopping list and the navigation cannot be clicked, tapped or tabbed to until the dialog closes.
- Keep a screen reader in the dialog too. Its own reading keys do not use Tab, so a Tab loop alone does not stop it from wandering into the week's grid. This is our own technical point, and it supports 4.1.2: what is announced as modal should be modal.
- Our own choice of technique: make the page behind inert, with the `inert` attribute or with the native `dialog` element opened with `showModal()` (see [H102](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/html/H102)), rather than rely on a script that catches Tab. Inert stops the keyboard, the pointer and assistive technology in one step.
- Mark the dialog as modal in code, with `aria-modal="true"` or the native element, only when the page behind really is out of use.
- Hold focus only in a dialog that can be closed from the keyboard. Escape does what *Cancel* does, as DLG-001 says, and *Cancel* is always there in a form, as FRM-003 says.
- The list of recipes under *Recipe* and the calendar under *Day* belong to the dialog, even if the code draws them elsewhere in the page. Focus may move into them and comes back to their control.
- The browser's own controls, such as the address bar, are not the page behind. With the native `dialog` element Tab may pass through them before it returns to the dialog, and that is fine.
- When one short confirmation sits over a full-screen dialog, as DLG-002 allows, the full-screen dialog counts as the page behind for as long as the confirmation is open.
- A press on the backdrop either takes the dismissive path in DLG-001 or does nothing. It never reaches a control underneath. In the adapter's `AlertDialog` the press does nothing, and focus stays where it was in the dialog. In `Dialog` the press closes the dialog, and focus goes back to the opener as DLG-006 says. If something holds the close, such as a discard question, the dialog stays open and focus stays where it was in it. Keeping focus in place on a press that does not close is our own choice.
- The adapter's `Dialog` and `AlertDialog` are modal: Radix, which they are built on, keeps Tab inside, and the content is marked `aria-modal="true"`. A dialog opened over another makes the one beneath inert. Do not pass `modal={false}` to `Dialog`: it turns the hold off while the content is still marked as modal. Check in your own build, with a screen reader, that nothing behind the dialog is read.

## Don't

- Let Tab leave *Plan a meal* after *Add to plan* and move through the week's grid behind the backdrop.
- Dim the page but leave its links open to a click, or the other way round: block the pointer and let Tab through.
- Catch Tab in a script and stop there, so a screen reader still reads the page behind and can press its buttons.
- Set `aria-modal="true"` on a panel that people are meant to work beside.
- Hold focus in a dialog that has no way out from the keyboard. That is the trap 2.1.2 forbids.
- Hold the keyboard in a layer that still lets the pointer use the page, and call it non-modal. Choose one: modal and closed off, or non-modal and free. An overlay panel that keeps focus inside until it is closed, as KBD-002 allows, counts as modal here, so the page behind it is out of use too.

## Exceptions

- **While sending.** Between the press on the main action and the reply, STA-005a may make *Cancel*, Escape and the close icon unavailable. That wait ends with the reply or with a failure the person can act on, so the dialog is not one without a way out.
- **Non-modal panels, toasts and tooltips.** A panel people work beside, a toast or a tooltip never holds focus. LAY-002 covers how the keyboard reaches a panel, and KBD-002 what a layer may cover.
- **A busy layer.** Content under a loading layer is made unreachable in the same way, but that moment belongs to STA-001.
- **Neighbouring rules.** Moving focus in and back is DLG-006. Leaving the dialog by keyboard is DLG-001 and KBD-001. One dialog at a time is DLG-002.
