## Rule

In a dialog that asks the person to confirm a destructive action, put initial focus on the Cancel button, never on the destructive button, and never let Enter run the destructive action unless the person has moved focus to that button.

## Why

The moment a dialog opens is the moment people are most likely to act without reading. Their hands are already on the keyboard, and Enter is the reflex that clears the way. If focus starts on the destructive button, or Enter fires it from anywhere in the dialog, that reflex deletes the project the person meant to keep. Starting on Cancel turns the dialog into a real pause: the person has to pick the destructive button on purpose, with Tab or a click, before anything is lost. [WCAG 2.2, 2.4.3 Focus Order](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#focus-order) asks that focus moves in an order that keeps meaning and use intact. It does not say which element in a dialog gets focus first, so this rule is our choice. The pause it gives also supports [3.3.4 Error Prevention (Legal, Financial, Data)](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#error-prevention-legal-financial-data) where that criterion applies.

## Our reasons

The judges are split on this, so we chose. We start focus on Cancel in every destructive confirmation, also when the person asked for the action, for three reasons.

- **A stray key press must not destroy data.** People press Enter out of habit, to clear whatever box is in the way, and sometimes by accident. A habit that saves a second must never cost a project, a customer list or a year of photos.
- **On the web, first focus decides what Enter does.** A web page has no system default button that Enter presses from anywhere. Enter acts on the element that has focus. Choosing where focus lands is therefore the same as choosing what Enter will do, and we choose the answer that changes nothing.
- **The cost is one key press.** A person who meant to delete presses Tab once and then Enter, or clicks the button. That small step is the price of the pause, and it falls only on people who have already decided.

## Do

- "Delete the project Autumn 2026?" opens with focus on *Cancel*. Enter now closes the dialog with nothing changed. To delete, the person moves to *Delete project* with Tab and presses Enter, or clicks it.
- Set initial focus on *Cancel* in code, for example with `autofocus`, rather than leaving it to whichever element comes first.
- Keep the buttons in the same order in every layout. If *Cancel* comes before *Delete project* in a row, it also comes first when the buttons stack on a small screen, in the code and on screen alike, as LAY-002 says.
- Give the dialog its title as its name and its question as its description, for example with `aria-labelledby` and `aria-describedby`, so a screen reader can read them when focus lands on *Cancel*.
- A dialog asks the person to type the project's name before *Delete project* works. Focus starts on *Cancel* as usual. Enter in the name field does nothing or moves focus on. It never runs the deletion, so the person still has to reach the button.
- Once the person has moved focus to *Delete project*, Enter and Space press it, as with any button.

## Don't

- Open the dialog with focus on *Delete project*, even when the person has just chosen *Delete* in a menu.
- Wrap the dialog in a form whose submit button is *Delete project*, so Enter in the name field deletes the project.
- Listen for Enter anywhere in the dialog and run the deletion, whatever has focus.
- Reverse the buttons when they stack, so *Delete project* comes first on a small screen. That breaks LAY-002, and focus left to the first button lands on the wrong one.
- Mark *Delete project* as the button Enter will press, with a ring or a hint, before the person has moved to it.
## Exceptions

- **Escape.** Escape does what Cancel does wherever focus is, as DLG-001 says.
- **Actions that can be undone.** When an action can be undone at once and no dialog is shown, this rule does not apply.
- **Labels and style.** What the buttons say is covered by DLG-003, and how the destructive button looks by DLG-005.
