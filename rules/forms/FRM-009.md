## Rule

Within one process, when a step needs information the person already entered or the service already showed them, either put it in the field or offer it on the same page to pick, and never make them type it again. Our own addition, which 3.3.7 does not ask for: a value put in the field stays editable.

## Why

Every time people type something twice, they spend time and effort on work the service could do, and they get a new chance to make a typo. The cost is highest for people with memory or motor impairments, for people who use voice control or a switch, and for anyone who finds it hard to copy text from one screen to the next. [WCAG 2.2, 3.3.7 Redundant Entry](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#redundant-entry) requires this, and the rule holds at that level (see [Understanding 3.3.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/redundant-entry.html)). A process runs from its first step to its last, including a step run by someone else, such as payment. It does not reach across visits, so nothing here asks you to keep data after the person leaves. The browser's own autofill is welcome, and `autocomplete` attributes help it ([WCAG 2.2, 1.3.5 Identify Input Purpose](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#identify-input-purpose)), but autofill alone does not meet the rule: the service itself has to bring the value back. Keeping a filled-in value editable is our own addition to the criterion.

## Do

- Booking a laundry slot, the person picks *Flat 1102* on the first page to see their building's laundry room. The next page needs the flat number for the door code, and the field already holds *1102*.
- Reporting a fault in a rented flat, the person gives a phone number for the first fault and then adds a second one. The contact question for the second fault offers *Same number as above, 07700 900123* as one choice and *A different number* as the other.
- Step 1 shows the case number *Case 2026-04417*. When a later step needs it, the field already holds it, or the step does not ask at all because the service knows it.
- Renewing a parking permit, the person uses the back control on the payment page to correct the registration number. The field opens with *ABC 123* still in it, and they change one character.
- Before sending, show the email address back with a way to change it: "We will send the confirmation to anna.berg@example.com. *Change email address*".
- When errors are shown, the values stay in their fields; FRM-002a and FRM-002b cover the errors themselves.
- A filled-in field keeps its visible label, as FRM-001 asks. Take care with personal data you bring back: show it only within the process that collected it.

## Don't

- Ask for *National ID number* in step 1 and again in step 4.
- Ask the person to copy a reference number from an earlier screen into a field.
- Set `autocomplete` on every field and treat the rule as met. Keep the attributes, but bring earlier values back yourself.
- Empty a field when the person goes back to it to change what they gave.
- Use the phone number from the first fault for the second one without showing it, so the person cannot see or change which number will be called.
- Add a second field, *Repeat email address*, to confirm the address. This is our reading of 3.3.7, which W3C does not name: show the address back for checking instead.

## Exceptions

Only three cases allow asking again:

- **Typing it again is the point of the task.** A safety course shows a checklist, then asks the person to write the steps from memory to pass. Filling them in would defeat the test.
- **It is needed for security.** When an account is created, a new password that is hidden as it is typed may be asked for a second time, as FRM-008 allows. A control to show the password is the friendlier way to let people check it, and you can offer both. A password typed in a failed sign-in may be cleared, and the service never puts a password back into a field itself; the person types it or their password manager fills it.
- **The earlier value no longer holds.** The time slot picked in step 1 was taken by someone else while the person filled in step 2, so they pick a new one.

Outside this rule: remembering answers between visits, lifting values out of a document the person uploaded, and a code sent to prove access to an email inbox, which is new information rather than a repeat. That code field should still accept paste and filling by the system, as FRM-008 describes. A field that asks for a value again, such as *Confirm new password*, still takes paste, as the guideline FRM-017 asks.
