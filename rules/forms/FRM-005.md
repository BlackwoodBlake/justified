## Rule

When a form marks required or optional fields, give every field of the same status the same marker, put it in the visible label or the group's legend as part of the accessible name, and explain a symbol in visible text no later than the first marked field.

## Why

People decide how to spend their effort on a form by what each field asks of them. If one field says "*", the next says "(required)" and a third says nothing, they cannot tell which fields they may skip, and they guess. A marker in the label is seen with the field's name, before anything is typed and after, and a screen reader reads it with that name. A star means nothing until someone says what it means, while a word explains itself. The rule helps meet [WCAG 2.2, 3.3.2 Labels or Instructions](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#labels-or-instructions) and [1.3.1 Info and Relationships](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#info-and-relationships). No WCAG criterion says which fields to mark or that a symbol needs a note.

## Do

- Put the marker right after the label text: "Email *" or "Phone (optional)". The marker is part of the label, so it is visible before, while and after the person fills in the field, as FRM-001 asks of the label itself.
- Pick one marker for each status the form marks and use it on every field and group of that status. If *Name* reads "Name *", so does *Email*, and so does every other field that must be filled in.
- Mark a group of radio buttons or checkboxes once, on its legend, such as "Delivery method *" over "Collect in store" and "Home delivery", not on each option.
- Explain a symbol in plain text the person meets no later than the first marked field. A line above the form, "Fields with * must be filled in", works. So does hint text at the first marked field, such as "* means the field must be filled in" under "Name *".
- Let a written marker such as "(optional)" or "(required)" stand on its own. A word needs no note.
- Keep the marker inside the label or legend, so it is part of the accessible name and a screen reader reads it with the field's name. A required state in code, such as `aria-required="true"`, is a good extra on top of that, never a stand-in for it. The `Field` component puts the written mark inside the label and sets `aria-required` when a field is required.
- Give the marker text contrast and the label's normal look, or a quiet one that still meets [1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum)](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#contrast-minimum). How to measure it is VIS-001.
- Use the same marker across forms of the same kind in a product, so a person who learned it on one sign-up form can read the next.

## Don't

- Mix markers in one form: "Name *" next to "Email (required)", or a star on some required fields and nothing on others.
- Put the marker only in the placeholder, as in an empty field reading "Required". It disappears at the first keystroke, and FRM-001 keeps essential information out of placeholders.
- Show a field's status only by colour, such as a red label for required fields, or only by an icon with no text and no accessible name.
- Rely on a note alone, such as a line above a booking form that names the fields to fill in while the fields themselves carry no marker. A note never replaces the marker at each field.
- Use a symbol and explain it only at the end of the form, in a tooltip, or not at all.
- Hide the marker from screen readers, for example with `aria-hidden` on the star, even when the field also sets `aria-required`.
- Draw the marker as an error, such as a red border, an error icon or a "Required field" message under the field. A marker says what a field asks for, not that it is wrong; when errors show is FRM-002b.
- Draw the star in a pale grey that is hard to see against the background.

## Exceptions

This rule does not decide which fields carry a marker: the required ones, the optional ones or both groups. The judges differ on that, and it is settled separately. Whichever choice a form makes, this rule covers how the marking works.

A form that marks no fields at all is outside this rule.
