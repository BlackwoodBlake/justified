## Rule

Give a field that asks only for an email address `type="email"`, a field for a phone number `type="tel"` and a field for a web address `type="url"`. A phone or tablet then shows a keyboard with the keys that value needs.

## Why

On a phone, the standard keyboard puts the at sign, the dot, the slash and the digits behind a second or third layout. Typing an email address or a phone number on it means switching layouts again and again, and every switch is a chance to hit the wrong key. The input type tells the browser what kind of value the field holds, and the browser picks a keyboard that has those keys on its first layout. On a computer with a physical keyboard the type changes nothing for typing.

The type is not the field's purpose. [WCAG 2.2, 1.3.5 Identify Input Purpose](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#identify-input-purpose) is met by the `autocomplete` value that FRM-007 asks for, never by the type. A field with the right type and no `autocomplete` value can still fail 1.3.5, and a field without the type does not fail it. The two go together: the type picks the keyboard, the `autocomplete` value lets the browser fill the field in.

## Our reasons

Fewer than two judges have spoken about which keyboard a field calls up, so the verdict is Insufficient evidence (1 of 3 judges spoke) and this guideline is our choice. Apple supports it. So does GOV.UK, an expert witness who does not vote. Material and Carbon are silent, which is not a vote against. We ask for the type for three reasons.

- **Fewer layout switches.** The right keys on the first layout save taps on every field, and they matter most to people with motor or dexterity difficulties, for whom each extra tap is slow and can go wrong.
- **It costs nothing.** The type is one attribute. It does not change how the field looks, and on a desktop it does nothing for typing.
- **Fewer typos in values that must be exact.** An email address or a phone number with one wrong character reaches nobody, and the person often finds out only when a confirmation never arrives. Keys that are easy to reach make that slip less likely.

## Do

- On the form *Become a supplier* for a weekly food market, *Email* is `<Input type="email" autoComplete="email" />`, *Mobile number* is `<Input type="tel" autoComplete="tel" />` and *Shop website* is `<Input type="url" autoComplete="url" />`. Each field keeps its visible label, as FRM-001 asks, and the `autocomplete` values follow FRM-007.
- Let *Mobile number* take spaces, brackets, dashes and a leading plus sign. `type="tel"` only picks the keyboard; it does not check the format. Check the value in your own code, as FRM-002a and FRM-002b describe.
- Our own advice: let *Shop website* take an address typed without `https://`, such as "fieldandfork.example", and add the scheme yourself. People seldom type it. Because `type="url"` makes the browser refuse an address without a scheme, this only works with the browser's own check turned off.
- Turn the browser's own check off on the form with `noValidate`, as FRM-007 says, and show errors at the field and after a failed send as FRM-002a, FRM-002b and FRM-011 describe.
- Keep `type="email"` on the field when a zod schema already checks the address. The schema checks the value; the type picks the keyboard. They do different jobs.
- Trim spaces from an email address in your own code before you check it. Some browsers trim them in an email field and some may refuse an address with letters outside ASCII; test the addresses your users have.

## Don't

- Leave *Email*, *Mobile number* and *Shop website* at the default text type, so every one opens the letters keyboard.
- Give *Mobile number* `type="number"`. A phone number is not a quantity; FRM-014b keeps it out of a number input.
- Use the type in place of the `autocomplete` value. *Email* with `type="email"` and no `autoComplete="email"` still lacks its purpose (FRM-007).
- Give *Email or username* on a sign-in form `type="email"`. The browser's check would refuse a user name, and the keyboard would suit only half of what the field takes.

## Exceptions

- **Fields that take more than one kind of value.** A field that accepts an email address or something else, such as a user name, stays `type="text"`.
- **Digits and numbers.** Fields that take only digits, such as codes and card numbers, are FRM-014a for the keypad and FRM-014b for the type. A phone number is covered here.
- **Search fields.** Whether a search field is `type="search"` is a question of role, not of keyboard, and is outside this guideline.
- **A product that chooses otherwise.** This is a guideline. A field without the type still needs its label (FRM-001) and its `autocomplete` value (FRM-007).
