## Rule

Give every page a title, in the `title` element, that says what the page holds or is for. In a single page app, set a new title on every route change, so the title names the view now shown, not only the app.

## Why

The title is what a browser tab, a bookmark and the history list show, and it is the first thing a screen reader says when a page opens. A person with ten tabs open finds the right one by its title. A screen reader user learns from it, before anything else, whether they have landed where they meant to. A title that says "React App" on every page tells them nothing, and one that never changes in a single page app gives no sign that the view has changed at all.

This is a requirement. [WCAG 2.2, 2.4.2 Page Titled](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#page-titled) asks that pages have titles that describe their topic or purpose. In HTML the title is the `title` element in `head` ([H25](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/html/H25)), and the Working Group's technique for a descriptive title asks that it names the subject and makes sense on its own ([G88](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G88)). The criterion's text does not mention single page apps. A route at its own address is its own web page, and the Working Group lists as a failure a title that a template repeats on every page, so the pages cannot be told apart by it ([F25](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F25)). For views that script swaps at one address, it also advises that the title change to match the current view ([Understanding 2.4.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/page-titled.html)). The criterion does not ask for a fixed format or, beyond that, for titles that are unique. The format under Do is our own choice.

## Do

- In the bookkeeping app *Fernhill*, the view at `/invoices` has the title "Invoices – Fernhill", and the view at `/invoices/2026-114` has "Invoice 2026-114 – Fernhill". Each route sets its own.
- In the Next.js App Router, export `metadata` or `generateMetadata` from each `page.tsx`, and set `title.template` to `"%s – Fernhill"` in the root layout so every page gets the same ending.
- In React 19, render `<title>` inside the route's component; React moves it into `head`. In React Router framework mode, use the route's `meta` export. In TanStack Router, use the route's `head` option. With any other router, set `document.title` when the route changes.
- Replace the starter title before the first release. Vite's `index.html` and `create-next-app` both ship one that names the tool, not the product.
- Our own choice: put the part that names the view first and the product name last, so tabs and screen reader announcements start with the words that differ.
- Our own choice: keep titles unique inside the product. Two views with the same title, such as two lists both called "Overview", get the words that tell them apart.
- Our own choice: make the title and the view's `h1` say the same thing, as far as the product name. The `h1` is STR-003.
- Our own choice: when a form is shown again with problems, start the title with "Error: ", as in "Error: New invoice – Fernhill", so a screen reader user hears it first. Where focus goes is still FRM-011.
- Our own choice: keep personal details of the person using the app out of the title, such as their email address, phone number or account balance. Titles travel further than the page: into history, shared screens and reports. The name of a record they work on, such as an invoice number, is fine.
- Our own advice: while a view loads its data, show a sensible title at once, such as "Invoice – Fernhill", and make it specific when the data arrives.
- Our own advice: let a link's text and the title of the page it opens say the same thing, so people can tell they arrived where they meant to. Link text itself is TXT-003.

## Don't

- Ship with "Vite + React + TS" or "Create Next App" as the title of every page.
- Set the title once in the root layout, so `/invoices`, `/customers` and `/settings` all read "Fernhill".
- Let a template placeholder leak into the title before data arrives, so the tab reads "undefined – Fernhill" or "{invoice.number} – Fernhill".
- Put the signed-in person's email address in the title, as in "anna.berg@example.com – Dashboard".
- Write a title that repeats the product's tagline on every page, so it describes the product and never the view.

## Exceptions

- **Dialogs and sheets.** A dialog or sheet that opens over a view does not need its own title. It has no route of its own, and its name comes from its heading.
- **Tabs inside a view.** Tabs that switch content inside one view keep the view's title. A tab that has its own route and its own heading is a view, and gets its own title.
- **Not covered here.** Whether focus moves or anything is announced after a route change is a separate question this rule does not decide.
- **Neighbouring rules.** The page's language, which changes along with the title when the interface switches language in place, is STR-006.
