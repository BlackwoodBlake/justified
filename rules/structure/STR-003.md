## Rule

Give each view one `h1` that names its main content. Give every other heading the level its place in the outline gives it: one below the heading it belongs under. Going down never skips a level; going back up may skip as many as needed.

## Why

Screen reader users often start a page by listing its headings, or by jumping to the first `h1` and then from level to level. The levels are their map of the page. One `h1` tells them where the main content starts and what it is. Levels that step down one at a time tell them which part belongs to which. A jump from `h2` to `h4` makes them wonder what they missed, and a view with no `h1`, or with three, gives them no clear place to begin. Whether text is a heading at all is STR-002a, and whether its words describe the part is STR-002b. This guideline only decides the level.

## Our reasons

Fewer than two judges have spoken about heading levels, so the verdict is Insufficient evidence (1 of 3 judges spoke) and this guideline is our choice. Material supports it. So does GOV.UK, an expert witness who does not vote. No WCAG criterion requires it. A second `h1` or a skipped level is not a documented failure. The Working Group's technique for organising a page with headings under 1.3.1 does nest them this way ([G141](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G141)), and so does its example of `h1` to `h6` ([H42](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/html/H42)). We follow it for three reasons.

- **The levels are a map only if they are kept.** People who move by level trust that an `h3` belongs to the `h2` above it. A level picked for any other reason sends them to the wrong place.
- **One start is easier than several.** "Jump to the `h1`" is a quick way into the main content, and it only works when there is exactly one.
- **It is cheap to get right and easy to check.** The level follows from the outline the team already has in mind. A linter or a heading list in the browser shows a gap at once.

## Do

- In the project tool *Ridgeway*, the view *Project settings* has one `h1`, *Project settings*. Its sections *Members*, *Billing address* and *Delete project* are `h2`. Inside *Members*, the parts *Pending invitations* and *Current members* are `h3`.
- Put the product name in the app shell's header as plain text or a link to the start page, not as an `h1`. The `h1` sits in each view, inside `main`, as STR-004 describes.
- On a dashboard, the section *This week* is an `h2`, and each card in its grid has an `h3` title, one below the section that holds it.
- After an `h4` deep in *Members*, the next section *Billing address* goes back to `h2`. Going back up any number of levels is fine.
- Let a reusable component take its heading level from where it is used. `SettingsSection` accepts `as="h2"` or `as="h3"`, or reads the level from a context the parent sets, so it fits both at the top of a view and nested inside another section.
- When the view shows rich text or Markdown that a user wrote, shift its headings down so its top heading sits below the view's `h1`. A comment's own `#` heading becomes an `h3` inside the section *Comments*, which is an `h2`.
- A dialog is its own context. `DialogTitle` is an `h2`, and that is fine whatever the page behind it uses. It is not a skip, and it need not be an `h1`.
- In the *Ridgeway* shell, the sidebar's list of projects may have its own `h2`, *Projects*. Where that sits in the outline next to the view's `h1` is free.
- The view's `h1` and its title usually say the same thing. The title is STR-001.

## Don't

- Build an app shell where the header shows a logo and every view starts at a `CardTitle` or an `h2`, so no view has an `h1`.
- Put the product name in an `h1` in the shared layout and another `h1` in each view.
- Follow the `h2` *Members* with an `h4` *Pending invitations*, with no `h3` between.
- Hard-code `<h3>` inside `SettingsSection`, so it is one level too deep at the top of a view and right only when nested.
- Show a user's Markdown with its `h1` intact inside a view that already has one.

## Exceptions

- **Content the team cannot change.** A third-party embed, such as a payment form or a map widget, may bring headings whose levels do not fit the view. Fix what you can around it; the embed itself is outside this guideline.
- **Dialogs.** A dialog's heading is outside the page's outline; see Do.
- **A product that chooses otherwise.** This is a guideline. A product that orders its levels another way still marks its headings in code and words them to describe, as STR-002a and STR-002b, both rules, ask.
