## Rule

Make every target that people press with a pointer at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels, or place it so that a circle 24 CSS pixels across, centred on its bounding box, touches no other target and no other small target's circle.

## Why

A small target is hard to hit for anyone with a tremor, stiff fingers, a shaky bus ride or a worn trackpad. A near miss either does nothing or, worse, presses the button next to it. This applies to every kind of pointer, so mouse, pen and touch all count, not only phones. [WCAG 2.2, 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum)](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#target-size-minimum) requires it at Level AA.

The size test is exact. An upright 24 by 24 CSS pixel square must fit entirely inside the area that responds to the pointer. A target that is smaller can still pass on spacing: draw a 24-pixel circle on the centre of its bounding box. If that circle reaches no other target, and does not overlap the circle of any other small target, the target passes. "Leave some room" is not enough; the circle is the test. Measure what can be pressed at the time of testing. While a slide-in *Cart* panel covers half of a product list, the hidden buttons are not measured; the buttons in the panel are, and so is whatever part of the list is still in view.

Our advice goes further than the law, and we mark it as advice only. For touch, aim for 44 CSS pixels or more, and give primary actions extra room, such as 48. The 44 figure is the size in [2.5.5 Target Size (Enhanced)](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#target-size-enhanced), which is Level AAA and not part of what WCAG 2.2 AA requires. The 48 figure is our own and does not come from WCAG. Prefer meeting 24 by size over leaning on the spacing test. Size is one measurement per target, with no neighbours to check, and it keeps passing when someone tightens the layout or it reflows on a narrow screen.

## Do

- Give an icon-only button such as *Edit* a hit area of at least 24 by 24, even when the pencil icon is 16. Padding around the icon counts, because it responds to the pointer.
- Measure the area that responds, not the drawing. Justified's Checkbox and RadioGroupItem each get a square target area of 24 by 24, even though the drawn box is smaller. The Switch's area is its 44 by 24 track grown by 2 pixels on every side, a rectangle of 48 by 28. All three meet the size test. Do not shrink them with a `className`, and keep the root font size at 16 pixels or more, or that area falls under 24.
- Check rounded shapes with the square, not the outline. A round *Like* button 24 pixels across fails the size test, because a 24-pixel square does not fit inside the circle. It can still pass on spacing when nothing else you can press is close to it. If something is, give it a square or larger hit area.
- A lone 16-pixel *i* button next to the heading *Delivery address* can pass on spacing. Its circle reaches 4 pixels past each side, so the button passes as long as no other target sits within that reach. If a link is added right beside it later, it fails, so a 24-pixel button is the safer choice.
- Treat a slider such as *Volume* as one target: the whole track responds, so measure the track. A separate drag handle, like the corner that resizes a *Comments* panel, is its own target and must pass on its own, and needs a way without dragging, as ACT-007a says.
- Keep the close icon in a dialog header, *Close*, at 24 or more, or clear of the other header actions by the circle test. DLG-001 ties Escape to that icon; the icon still has to be easy to press.
- When the viewport is narrow, let a button row wrap instead of shrinking its targets or closing their gaps. At 320 CSS pixels, LAY-001 and this rule apply at once.
- Offer a compact layout, such as a denser table for *Invoices*, only as a choice the person turns on, and keep the control that turns it off at full size.

## Don't

- Stack *Increase* and *Decrease* arrows in a quantity field, each 24 wide and 10 tall, directly on top of each other. Each arrow's circle runs into the other arrow.
- Number the pages of a search result *1*, *2*, *3* as 16-pixel links with 2 pixels between them. Their circles overlap.
- Shrink a checkbox's target under 24 and rely on its label to make it pass, unless the label itself meets 24 by 24 or the spacing test. The visible label from FRM-001 counts as the same control only when it passes on its own.
- Argue that a target is fine because it grows when people zoom in. CSS pixel sizes stay the same at any zoom, so zoom never turns a fail into a pass.
- Let two targets overlap and count the shared part toward both, unless they do the same thing.
- Turn on a dense mode by default that pushes buttons under 24.

## Exceptions

- **Inline.** A target that sits in a line of text and takes its size from that text is outside the rule, such as the link *terms* in "I accept the terms".
- **Equivalent.** A small control is excused when another control on the same page does the same job and passes. A compact *Share* icon on each card is fine if a full-size *Share* button for the same item is also on the page.
- **User agent.** A control the browser draws at its default size, such as an unstyled native radio button, is excused while you leave its size alone. Once you restyle it, for example to make it smaller, it must pass.
- **Essential.** When the size or position carries the meaning, or a law sets the layout, the target is excused. Offer a larger alternative where you can.
