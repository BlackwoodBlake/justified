## Rule

Everything people click or tap to go somewhere or to act has a control role in code, such as link or button. Something shown as a link whose only job is to take people to another address has the link role.

## Why

A screen reader tells people what each thing is before they use it: "link", "button", "menu item". It also lets them jump through a list of every link or every button on the page. A `div`, `span` or image with a click handler is none of these. It has no control role: it is read as plain text, if it is reached at all, and it is missing from those lists, so the person may never learn that it does anything. [WCAG 2.2, 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#name-role-value) requires that every control, scripted ones included, has a role software can work out. The Working Group lists a generic element made clickable by script without a role as a failure ([F59](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F59)). It also fails anything presented as a link that reaches its target by script while its role is not link ([F42](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F42)), even when it works from the keyboard. An anchor with no `href` is not a link in HTML, so it has no control role either. The criterion asks for the role, not for a native element; building with `<button>` and `<a href>` is the Working Group's advice and ours. That every control also works from the keyboard ([2.1.1 Keyboard](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#keyboard)) is KBD-001. [1.3.1 Info and Relationships](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#info-and-relationships) supports this rule, since a fake link loses what the page shows about it.

## Do

- In a library's app, *My loans* shows each book as a shadcn `Card`. The book's title is a `Link` to its page and *Renew loan* is a `Button`. A click elsewhere on the card may open the book's page as a shortcut, as KBD-001 allows.
- Under the opening hours, *All branches* looks like a text link and is one: `<Button variant="link" asChild><Link href="/branches">All branches</Link></Button>` renders an `<a href>`.
- The star that adds a book to *Wish list* is a `Button size="icon"` with the lucide `Star` inside, named as ACT-001b says.
- Wrap a real `Button` in `DialogTrigger`, `DropdownMenuTrigger` or `PopoverTrigger` when you use `asChild`. Without `asChild` they render a `<button>` themselves.
- Our advice, which is also the Working Group's: use `<button>` and `<a href>`. The adapter's `Button` renders `<button type="button">`, or the element you give it with `asChild`, and Next.js and React Router `Link` render `<a href>`.
- If a control must be built by hand, it needs all of it: `role="button"` or `role="link"`, `tabIndex={0}`, Enter (and Space for a button), `aria-disabled` when it is off, any other state it has (VIS-008a), and a name (ACT-001b). Enter and Space are our advice; keyboard use is KBD-001. That can meet the criterion, but it is the worse way.
- A link that downloads a file, such as *Download loan history (CSV)*, is still a link: `<a href="/loans.csv" download>`.
- Which of the two to pick for a given job is ACT-009a for going somewhere and ACT-009b for acting in place.

## Don't

- Put `onClick={() => router.push("/books/" + book.id)}` on the `Card` and nothing inside it that is a link.
- Write `<a onClick={renewLoan}>Renew loan</a>` with no `href`. It has no role in HTML and takes no focus.
- Build *All branches* as `<Button variant="link" onClick={() => router.push("/branches")}>`. It looks like a link and goes somewhere, but a screen reader calls it a button and it is not in the list of links.
- Make *Late fees* an underlined `span` with `cursor-pointer` that calls `navigate("/fees")`.
- Put `onClick` straight on the lucide `Star` icon.
- Put a `span` inside `PopoverTrigger asChild`, so the trigger loses the button it would have rendered.

## Exceptions

- **A pointer shortcut.** A click on a card, a row or a backdrop may do what a real control nearby already does. The control carries the role; the shortcut needs none. KBD-001 and TBL-003 say the same.
- **Act, then move.** A control whose job is to save, send, delete or sign out, or send typed input such as *Search*, stays a button when a page follows, also when it looks like a link (see ACT-009a).
- **Table cells and rows.** Do not put `role="button"` or `role="link"` on a `th`, `tr` or `TableRow` to make it clickable. Put the control inside, as TBL-002 and TBL-003 say.
- **Neighbouring rules.** The keyboard half is KBD-001, the name is ACT-001b, and link text is TXT-003.
