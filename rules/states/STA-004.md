## Rule

When the product sets a time limit that can end a person's chance to act or throw away their work, let them turn it off, lengthen it, or extend it after a warning, unless an exception applies. Each option meets 2.2.1 only with the conditions listed for it first under Do. Our advice is to prefer them in that order.

## Why

Some people read slowly, type with a switch or a head pointer, use a screen reader, or need to stop and think. A clock they cannot see or control can sign them out halfway through and take their work with it. [WCAG 2.2, 2.2.1 Timing Adjustable](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#timing-adjustable) requires one of the three options for every limit the content sets. A limit that exists for security, or that the product's own server enforces, still counts. It covers an idle sign-out, a session with a fixed maximum length, a hold on something the person has reserved, a step that must be done within a set time, and a page that refreshes or redirects on its own after a delay. No limit at all helps most, so ask first whether the limit is needed. A warning at the end helps least, because it still interrupts.

The warning has to reach everyone. If it appears without taking focus, it is a status message under [WCAG 2.2, 4.1.3 Status Messages](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#status-messages), and FBK-004 says how it is announced; losing work soon is the kind of warning FBK-004 keeps role="alert" for. The control that extends the limit must work from the keyboard, as [WCAG 2.2, 2.1.1 Keyboard](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#keyboard) asks.

We also aim, beyond Level AA, for two things that WCAG places at Level AAA and does not require here. When a person signs in again after a lapse, they carry on where they were with nothing lost ([WCAG 2.2, 2.2.5 Re-authenticating](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#re-authenticating)). And when idle time can lose their data, they are told how long that time is when the task starts ([WCAG 2.2, 2.2.6 Timeouts](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#timeouts)). These are our aims, not part of the requirement.

## Do

- **Option 1: turn it off.** The person can switch the limit off before they reach it.
- **Option 2: lengthen it.** Before they reach it, the person can set the limit to at least ten times the default, over a wide range.
- **Option 3: warn and extend.** Before time runs out, warn the person, give them at least 20 seconds to answer, and let them extend the limit with one simple action, such as one button. Allow that at least ten times.
- An internal admin tool signs people out after 30 minutes of inactivity. Under *Settings*, *Security*, the person can choose *Sign me out after* 30 minutes, 2 hours, 8 hours or *Never on this device*. The longest choice is far more than ten times the default.
- A web app signs people out after 15 minutes idle. Two minutes before that, a dialog with role="alertdialog" opens: "You will be signed out in 2 minutes because you have been inactive." It has two buttons, *Stay signed in* and *Sign out*. One press of *Stay signed in* resets the clock, and the person can do that as often as they like.
- The warning stays on screen until the person answers or the limit passes. It is never a toast that closes on a timer (FBK-005 and FBK-003 cover how long messages stay).
- A person is typing in a dialog *Edit invoice* when the idle warning is due. No second dialog opens. A notice appears inside *Edit invoice*, above its buttons, with *Stay signed in*, the same way DLG-002 shows a failed main action. Focus stays where it was, and the notice is announced as FBK-004 says.
- When no dialog is open, the warning may instead be an alert in the page with role="alert" and a *Stay signed in* button the person can reach by keyboard. It does not cover the element that has focus (KBD-002).
- Pressing Escape on the warning dialog counts as *Stay signed in*. That is the button that leaves everything as it is, so DLG-001 gives it to Escape. It resets the clock and closes the warning. Escape never signs the person out, and it never just hides the warning while the clock keeps running.
- Our own choice: give the warning dialog only *Sign out* and *Stay signed in*, with no *Cancel*, *Close* or close icon. A third way out would leave the person unsure whether the clock is still running.
- After *Stay signed in*, focus returns to where the person was, and every field keeps what they typed. Extending never discards unsaved work, also in a full-screen dialog with unsaved changes (FRM-004a).
- A support chat ends the conversation after five minutes without a reply from the person. A minute before that, a line in the chat says "This chat will close in 1 minute because we have not heard from you." with a button *Keep the chat open*. Each press gives five more minutes, and the line stays until the person answers or the chat closes.
- A confirmation page shows "Your password has been changed" and a link *Go to your account*. It does not redirect on its own after five seconds. If a redirect is truly needed, let the person stop it or follow the link when they are ready.
- Our aim beyond AA: the first step of a long customer survey says, once, "If you leave this survey for 45 minutes, it closes. Your answers so far are kept." When the person opens the survey link again, they return to the question they were on with their answers in place (FRM-009).
- Our own choice: when the product runs in several tabs, the warning and the extension apply to the whole session, so choosing *Stay signed in* in one tab keeps the others signed in too.

## Don't

- Sign a person out after a period of inactivity with no warning, no setting and no way to extend.
- Show a warning only 10 seconds before the limit, or show a countdown with no button to get more time.
- Make the person type their password or solve a puzzle to extend the limit. One simple action is enough.
- Allow only one or two extensions and then sign the person out anyway.
- Show the warning as a toast that disappears after a few seconds.
- Open the warning as a second dialog on top of a dialog the person is working in.
- Throw away what the person typed when they extend the limit, or reload the page to do it.
- Let Escape or closing the warning count as *Sign out*.
- Refresh a page of open orders every two minutes on a timer while the person is reading or filling it in, with no way to stop it.
- Treat a limit as essential just because it is convenient for the server.

## Exceptions

- **Real-time events.** When a live event cannot happen without the limit and no alternative is possible, such as a live poll that closes when the presenter moves to the next question, the three options do not apply.
- **Essential limits.** When more time would break the point of the activity, the limit may stay, for example a timed reaction drill in a training game. Some security limits can count as essential, such as a sign-in code that lapses after a few minutes; the sign-in still follows FRM-008. Even then, move as much of the work as possible out of the timed part.
- **Limits longer than 20 hours.** These are outside the requirement.
- **Limits outside the product.** A limit set by the browser, the operating system or the network is not one the product sets. A limit the person chose themselves meets the second option only if the setting reaches at least ten times the default.
- **Short-lived messages.** How long a toast or snackbar stays on screen, including one that holds a button, is covered by FBK-005 and FBK-003, not by this rule.
- **Limits on reading.** Content that moves or updates on its own, such as a carousel, a ticker or a live feed, is outside this rule. [WCAG 2.2, 2.2.1 Timing Adjustable](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#timing-adjustable) and [WCAG 2.2, 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#pause-stop-hide) still apply to it; VIS-005 covers the second.
- **After the limit passes.** What the person sees once they have been signed out follows FBK-002, which says that news is never carried only by a message that closes itself.
