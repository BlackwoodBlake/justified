## Rule

After an action succeeds, the item or value it changed shows its new state in the view. If the result has no place in the view, leave a lasting sign near the control or somewhere the person can return to. A toast that closes by itself is never the only sign.

## Why

People need to know that what they did worked. A toast or snackbar that fades after a few seconds is easy to miss: the person looked at the field they were typing in, read slowly, zoomed in on another part of the screen or turned away for a moment. When they miss it, they either do the same thing again, and send the message twice, or stop to check whether it worked. A view that shows the change itself answers the question for as long as the person needs: the row is gone, the switch is on, the new name is in the heading. A toast can still say so as well; it just cannot carry the news alone. No WCAG criterion asks the view itself to change, and none asks for a success message at all, so this rule rests on the judges. It does help with [WCAG 2.2, 4.1.3 Status Messages](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#status-messages): it concerns blind and low vision people who use screen readers, and they are among those a passing toast fails most often. How a message reaches assistive technology is covered by FBK-004. A toast that vanishes on a timer can also work like a time limit, most clearly when it holds a button, and showing the result in the view keeps people clear of that, in the spirit of [2.2.1 Timing Adjustable](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#timing-adjustable).

## Do

- The person renames a folder to *Receipts 2026*. The folder's name in the list and in the heading changes at once. A toast saying "Folder renamed" is a fine extra.
- The person turns on *Show week numbers* in the calendar settings. The switch shows on, and week numbers appear in the calendar. Nothing more is needed.
- The person deletes the row *Invoice 2026-0412*. The row leaves the list at once, and a toast offers *Undo*. The toast stays until the person uses *Undo*, closes it or leaves the view, as DST-001a says. If they undo, the row comes back in the same place and in view. The invoice can also be restored from *Deleted items*, which lasts after the toast is gone.
- The person sends a message from a form that stays on screen. Next to *Send* a lasting line reads "Sent at 14:02 to Anna Brooks", and the message is listed under *Sent*.
- The person exports a report that is built in the background. The export button area says "Creating export …" and then "Ready: report-november.csv" with a link, and the file is also listed under *Exports*, where it stays.
- The main action in a dialog succeeds and the dialog closes. The page behind it shows the saved values, and a message on the page such as "Profile updated" may say so too, as DLG-002 describes.

## Don't

- Save a setting and show only "Settings updated!" in a toast that fades after four seconds, while the form still looks exactly as before.
- Move a card to *Done* on a board and leave it in the old column until the page is reloaded, with a toast as the only sign it moved.
- Send an invitation and show only a toast "Invitation sent", with no lasting trace near the button and no list of sent invitations to check later.
- Run a long import in the background and report that it finished only in a toast that may close before the person comes back to the tab.

## Exceptions

- **Toasts that stay until closed.** The last sentence of the rule is about toasts that close by themselves. A toast that stays until the person uses it, closes it or leaves the view, such as the message with *Undo* in DST-001a, is not limited by it. The first two sentences still apply: the changed item shows its new state, and a result with no place in the view still gets its lasting sign.
- **When the view is the confirmation.** If the action takes the person to a page that shows the result, such as the new item's own page, that page is the lasting sign and no extra message is needed. The same goes for a page that opens with a lasting message about the result, such as "Your application has been sent" at the top, kept until the person leaves the page.
- **Failures and announcements.** This rule covers success only. Where failures are shown is covered by FBK-002, and how messages reach assistive technology by FBK-004.
- **Actions that can be undone.** DST-001b sets when an action that can be undone runs at once instead of after a question, and DST-001a how *Undo* is offered and how long it stays. DLG-003, DLG-004 and DLG-005 let such an action skip the confirmation dialog. The message with *Undo* follows this rule: the change still shows in the view, for example the archived project leaves the list.
