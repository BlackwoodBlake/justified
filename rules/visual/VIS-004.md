## Rule

The parts people need to see to find a control and tell its state, and the parts of a graphic they need to understand it, reach 3:1 against the colours next to them. Inactive controls, decoration and graphics whose information is also given in text are exempt.

## Why

A text field nobody can see is a field nobody fills in. A checkbox whose box fades into the card, a switch whose track blends with the panel, or a chart whose lines are pale on white leaves people guessing what is there and what state it is in. [WCAG 2.2, 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#non-text-contrast) requires 3:1 for the visual information needed to identify a control and its state, and for the parts of a graphic needed to understand it. It is measured against the colours that touch the part. It does not ask for a border on everything: a button with readable text is found by its text, so its container need not reach 3:1. A text field has no text until someone types, so its edge, or a fill that stands out from the page, is what shows it. State marks count too: the tick in a checkbox, the dot in a radio button, the thumb and track of a switch. These may be measured against another part of the control, such as the tick against the box's fill. The criterion does not compare a control's states with each other; a state that differs only by hue is VIS-003a. The exception for controls the browser draws unchanged hardly ever applies in a shadcn app, where every control is styled by the author. Where a product has a light and a dark theme, we measure in both. People meet either one, so we treat each as the page; that is our way of applying the criterion, not words in it.

## Do

- Edge fields with `border-input`, as Justified's `Input`, `Textarea`, `NativeSelect`, `Checkbox` and `RadioGroup` do. The contrast test checks `input` against the page at 3:1 in both themes. Measured further, it gives about 3.3:1 on `muted` and 3.7:1 on `card` in the light theme, and 3.5:1 and 4.2:1 in the dark one, so a field on those panels still passes. Which border token to use is VIS-002.
- Use Justified's `Switch` as it is: the track is `bg-input` when off and `bg-primary` when on, with a thumb in the page colour, and both states keep 3:1.
- Give an icon-only button's icon 3:1 against what is behind it. On an energy dashboard's toolbar, the *Export readings* button with a download icon keeps the icon in `text-foreground` and its name in code (ACT-001b).
- In a bar chart of *Daily use in kWh*, give each bar 3:1 against the chart's background.
- On an account page, a storage meter split into *Photos*, *Videos* and *Documents* shows each segment at 3:1 against the track. Neighbouring segments either differ by 3:1 from each other, or have a 2 px gap in the background colour between them, so each part can be seen. The meter alone tells how full the account is, so it needs the contrast; the exact sizes may also stand in text next to it.
- Check a product's own chart palette in both themes; shadcn's `--chart-1` to `--chart-5` are not in Justified's test.
- Give a status dot 3:1 against the page. Justified's status colours are tested at 4.5:1 as text on the page, so as dots on the page they pass. If a product's own status colour is too light as a dot, darken it, or give the dot an edge that reaches 3:1 against the page.
- Draw the indicator of a selected tab or row, such as an underline bar or a check, at 3:1 against what is next to it, when that indicator is how people see the state.
- Measure the centre of a part drawn with a gradient against what surrounds it.

## Don't

- Edge an `Input`, a custom select, a combobox trigger or a date picker button with `border-border`. It measures about 1.4:1 against the light page and 1.5:1 against the dark one.
- Make a field `border-transparent bg-muted/50`, or `border-0 bg-transparent` in a toolbar or a table cell, so that only the placeholder shows where it is. The placeholder is not the field's edge and is gone once someone types.
- Draw an unchecked checkbox with `border-muted-foreground/30`, put a `bg-muted` switch track on a `bg-muted` card, or draw a slider rail in `bg-secondary` on the page.
- Put `opacity-50` on an icon button or toggle that still works to mean "off" or "less important". The exemption is for inactive controls only.
- Draw an icon-only button's icon in `text-muted-foreground/50`, or a light icon on a tinted toolbar.
- Add a `dark:` override for a control's fill and forget its border.
- Rely on a tooltip or a hover highlight to make chart lines visible. The chart at rest must pass.

## Exceptions

- **Inactive controls.** A control that cannot be used at that moment. When to switch a control off at all is ACT-004 and ACT-005.
- **Information in text.** A graphic whose information is also given as text, such as values printed on each bar or a table of the same data, need not reach 3:1 in its parts. The text then follows VIS-001.
- **Decoration and pictures.** Decoration, photos and illustrations people do not need in order to understand the content.
- **Hover.** A hover fill is not a state people must identify, so it need not reach 3:1.
- **Text.** Text, including axis labels and values in a chart, is VIS-001.
- **Neighbouring rules.** The focus indicator is KBD-001, and focus seen through a translucent layer KBD-002. States and statuses told apart by colour only are VIS-003a.
